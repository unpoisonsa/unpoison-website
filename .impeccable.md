## Design Context

### Users

UnPoison serves several audiences with different levels of urgency and prior knowledge:

- donors assessing whether the organisation is credible, active, and worth supporting;
- policymakers, researchers, academics, journalists, and partner organisations looking for evidence, policy submissions, reports, databases, and authoritative context;
- affected residents and communities looking for guidance or a way to report pesticide spraying and poisoning incidents;
- farmers and practitioners looking for safer alternatives, practical resources, and policy information;
- UnPoison's own maintainers, principally the project owner and Lyle, who need to publish and reorganise content without returning to a page-builder workflow.

The central jobs are to understand what UnPoison does, find reliable information quickly, follow current work and campaigns, report an incident, get involved, or donate. Visitors may arrive curious, sceptical, concerned, time-constrained, or distressed, so the site must establish trust quickly and provide obvious routes without forcing everyone through the same long narrative.

The primary visitor sequence is **understand, then act**: first grasp who UnPoison is, why the work matters, and why the organisation is credible; then choose an appropriate action such as exploring evidence, joining a campaign, reporting an incident, offering expertise, contacting the organisation, or donating.

### Brand Personality

The brand is **credible, informed, and proactive**.

The interface and writing should feel evidence-led and serious without becoming bureaucratic, alarmist, or fatalistic. It should communicate that UnPoison understands the science and policy landscape, acts constructively, and gives people practical ways to participate. The emotional outcome should be confidence in the organisation and a sense that meaningful action is possible.

### Aesthetic Direction

Preserve and refine the recognisable UnPoison identity rather than replacing it. Retain the existing logo/canary imagery, established colour cues, photographic character, typographic heritage, and the “For All Forever” line, while improving consistency, accessibility, information hierarchy, and performance.

The initial site is English-first and primarily light-themed, matching research, reading, daylight/mobile, and public-interest use. The visual system should feel editorial and civic with an organic South African character: authoritative enough for policy work, human enough for affected communities, and energetic enough for advocacy.

Explicit anti-directions: generic NGO template, corporate sustainability microsite, overly academic archive, fear-driven or alarmist campaign page, and overly rustic or handmade environmental branding.

### Design Principles

1. **Evidence before decoration.** Make current work, reports, sources, dates, authorship, and document metadata easy to verify and navigate.
2. **Many audiences, clear routes.** Give each major audience an obvious path while keeping the organisation's purpose legible at a glance.
3. **Preserve identity, remove friction.** Keep recognisable UnPoison assets and tone while replacing the long-page and page-builder complexity with a coherent content system.
4. **Fast on ordinary connections.** Prioritise low-bandwidth and lower-end mobile performance; avoid eager video embeds, excessive font payloads, and unnecessary client-side JavaScript.
5. **Accessible by default.** Target WCAG 2.2 AA, keyboard operation, meaningful alternative text, reduced-motion support, readable line lengths, strong focus states, and resilient layouts.
6. **Content parity without structural imitation.** Preserve valuable public information and URLs where appropriate, but freely reorganise, consolidate, archive, and redirect content when that improves comprehension.
7. **Maintainable through simple files.** Use structured Markdown/content files and version control so technical maintainers and LLM-assisted workflows can update the site safely and transparently.

### Delivery Constraints

- Launch as soon as responsibly possible.
- Content parity is required, but the existing structure is not.
- Moderate editorial pruning is acceptable.
- Donation-system redesign is deferred; preserve the existing capability during the initial migration.
- Incident-report handling is not yet understood; preserve or bridge the existing form until privacy, storage, notification, and operational requirements are confirmed.
- Initial language: English.
- Primary maintainers are technical and comfortable with Markdown and Git-based workflows.
